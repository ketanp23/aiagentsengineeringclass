2 · The pipeline you're building

Skills, sub-agents & judgement

A Skill is standing instructions; a sub-agent is a helper Cowork spins up on its own. The twist today: the second Skill isn't more analysis — it's the judgement that finds what matters.

In one line each: a Skill is a note-to-self Cowork reads automatically — "analyse VTR this way", "decide what's meaningful that way." A sub-agent is a separate worker the main agent hands a chunk of the job to, then gets a clean summary back. You write Skills; Cowork creates sub-agents for you.
Skillstanding note
A folder with a SKILL.md file. Its description is the trigger; its body is the recipe. Installed once, reused forever.
Lumen Reach examplecampaign-vtr-analysis tells Cowork to compute VTR for every dimension and combination — always with impressions attached.
Sub-agenta helper
A separate worker with its own context. You don't define them — you ask for parallel work and the lead agent splits it up.

The judgementsignal vs noise
The point of the session: a finding only counts if it's Material (enough volume), Reliable (not small-sample luck) and Actionable (maps to a decision).
Lumen Reach exampleA 91% VTR on 40 impressions fails "Material" and "Reliable" — it's noise, and gets cut, however good it looks.
The trap this session is about AI ranks by whatever you point it at. Ask "highest VTR" and it will float tiny, lucky placements to the top. A rate means nothing without the volume behind it.
Looks amazing: 91% VTR · 40 impressions → delivers nothing
Actually matters: 68% VTR · 200,000 impressions → moves the goal

The filter: three tests every insight must pass

Test 1

Material

Enough volume to move the goal. A great rate on tiny impressions changes nothing. Apply a volume floor and say what it is.

Test 2

Reliable

Real signal, not small-sample luck. A ratio on a small denominator is untrustworthy — high rate + low volume is a trap.

Test 3

Actionable

Maps to a decision — scale it, cut it, shift budget. An interesting fact that changes no action is trivia.

Why put the judgement in a Skill?Because "always carry impressions with the rate," "apply a volume floor," and "don't double-count overlapping segments" are exactly the rules people forget under time pressure. Baked into insight-review, they run every time — so the brief that reaches the client is disciplined by default, not by luck.

4 · The two prompts

Your prompts for the run

Two prompts, run back to back in the same Cowork task. The first gets the raw analysis out of the AI; the second is where you find the meaningful insights. Fill the blanks yourself, then open the solution to compare.

PROMPT 1

Get the AI analysis

Your prompt — fill the blanks Analyse the attached Lumen Reach campaign data. Use  what kind of helpers?  to compute VTR by  which dimensions?  in parallel, then scan  which combinations? .
For every combination show  which two numbers together?  and a  what tag? .
Don't pre-filter —  surface what? .
Put the campaign's  which baseline?  at the top. Deliver  which file? .
Show solution prompt
Paste into Cowork Analyse the attached Lumen Reach video campaign data. Use sub-agents to compute VTR by each dimension — Exchange, Environment, Video Player Size, Device Type, City, App/URL — in parallel, then scan 2-, 3- and 4-dimension combinations.

For every combination, show impressions AND VTR together, and tag each by volume (High / Medium / Low). Don't pre-filter to the good ones — surface everything with its volume so we can judge it in the next step.

Put the campaign's overall (blended) VTR and total impressions at the top as the baseline to beat. Deliver an Excel workbook with an Overview sheet and a Combinations sheet.

This is the "get it all out" step. It will produce a long list — including the seductive tiny-volume, high-VTR rows. That's expected; you filter them next.

PROMPT 2

Find the meaningful insights

Your prompt — fill the blanks From that analysis, find the  which kind of?  insights.
Restate the goal in numbers:  what target? .
Apply a  what threshold?  and drop everything below it — tell me  how many it removed? .
Separate  what from what? ; call out the  which trap?  you're excluding.
Build a shortlist that  does what cumulatively?  with a running total, plus a  what list?  of what to cut. Finally,  check the AI for what? .
Show solution prompt
Paste into Cowork From that analysis, find the meaningful insights — the ones that are material, reliable and actionable. First restate the goal in numbers: assemble strategies that together deliver at least 2,450,000 impressions at a blended VTR of at least 62%, against the current ~54%.

Apply a volume floor — ignore any combination under ~20,000 impressions — and tell me how many candidates that removed. Separate signal from noise: explicitly call out the high-VTR but tiny-volume placements you're excluding, and why.

Build a shortlist that cumulatively clears the target, with a running total (strategy · impressions · VTR · cumulative impressions · blended VTR), and stop when the target is met — no double-counting overlapping segments. Add a stop-list of high-volume, low-VTR segments to pause. Finally, audit the analysis: flag anything it over-claimed, double-counted, or asserted without a number. Deliver a one-page Meaningful Insights brief.

The volume floor is the single move that kills the traps. The running total proves the goal is actually met, and the audit line makes the AI check its own homework — the heart of "meaningful insights from AI outputs."
