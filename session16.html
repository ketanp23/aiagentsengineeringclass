2 · The pipeline you're building

Skills vs sub-agents

Two ideas do all the work. A Skill is standing instructions; a sub-agent is a helper Cowork spins up on its own.

In one line each: a Skill is a note-to-self Cowork reads automatically — "when you see sales data, analyse it this way and lay the report out that way." A sub-agent is a separate worker with its own scratch space that the main agent hands a chunk of the job to, then gets a clean summary back. You write Skills; Cowork creates sub-agents for you.
Skillstanding note
A folder with a SKILL.md file. Its description is the trigger; its body is the recipe. Installed once, reused forever.
Northwind examplesales-analysis-report tells Cowork which breakdowns to run and what the report must contain — every quarter, identically.
Sub-agenta helper
A separate worker with its own context. You don't define them — you ask for parallel work and the lead agent splits it up.
Northwind exampleRegion, product, rep and segment breakdowns each go to their own sub-agent and run at the same time; only results come back.
Chainingstep to step
One Skill's output feeds the next. Run them in the same Cowork task so step 2 can see step 1's numbers and charts.
Northwind exampleThe board-deck Skill reuses the workbook's figures instead of recomputing — same numbers on the slides as in the analysis.
House styleconsistency
Because the rules live in the Skill, the output looks the same whoever runs it and whenever they run it.
Northwind exampleNext quarter, or a colleague on another client, drops in a new CSV and gets the same board-ready format.
Why a Skill instead of just asking?If you only ever did this once, a plain prompt is fine. The Skill earns its keep on repetition and on governance — the "exclude returns", "every claim needs a number", "keep it at board altitude" rules are baked in, so the output doesn't drift from person to person or quarter to quarter.

Your prompts for the run

Two prompts, run back to back in the same Cowork task. Fill the blanks in yourself first, then open the solution to compare.

PROMPT 1

Analyse the data

Your prompt — fill the blanks Run our standard  which report?  on the attached Northwind sales data.
Use  what kind of helpers?  to work the  which breakdowns?  in parallel.
Clean first —  how to handle returns / dropped rows? .

I want  which sections + a chart of…?  and  how many actions? .

Show solution prompt
Paste into Cowork Run our standard sales performance report on the attached Northwind Devices sales data. Use sub-agents to work the region, product, rep and customer-segment breakdowns in parallel, then assemble one report.

Clean first: exclude returned orders from revenue, and tell me how many rows you dropped and why. Focus the period read on Q2 2026 (Apr–Jun) vs the prior quarter and vs the same quarter last year.

I want an executive summary, headline metrics, a monthly revenue trend chart, breakdowns by region / product / rep / segment, and three recommended actions. Deliver it as an Excel workbook — a summary sheet plus one sheet per breakdown — and state the date range and row count at the top.

Naming the breakdowns is what triggers the parallel sub-agents. Pinning the period to Q2 2026 vs prior-Q and YoY is what makes it board-relevant rather than a generic dump.

Paste into Cowork Now turn that report into a board deck for Northwind's C-suite. Reuse the numbers and charts you just produced — don't recompute.

Details to use: company is Northwind Devices; this is the Q2 2026 board meeting on 1 September 2026; presenter is Priya Raman, CFO; and the ask for the board is approval to shift two sales headcount from West to East given the regional trend, plus approval for a Q4 inventory build ahead of the seasonal spike.

Keep it at board altitude — one idea per slide, headline titles that state the conclusion, a KPI scorecard with vs-prior-quarter and YoY deltas, a risks-and-mitigations slide, and an explicit "decisions needed" slide. Put speaker notes on every content slide, including the likely board question and its answer. Deliver a .pptx.

Because both Skills are installed and named clearly, Cowork loads board-deck here and reuses the workbook's figures — same numbers on the slides as in the analysis.
