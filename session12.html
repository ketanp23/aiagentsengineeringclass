Part 2 · What you built

The three Session 07 Skills

These are the three Skills you'll be evaluating today. Each is a folder with a SKILL.md that Cowork runs. Here's what each one is supposed to do.

Track A

weekly-status-update

Turns raw engagement notes into a Harbourstone client status update in the house format — seven fixed sections, a RAG status, outcome first.

The hard part: never invent a number (mark gaps [TBC]), keep it client-safe (no internal cost/margin, no unflattering staff notes), and stay in its lane (decline non-status asks).

Track B

swot-analysis

Turns a company brief into a structured, evidence-based SWOT: four quadrants (Strengths · Weaknesses · Opportunities · Threats) plus an Evidence-gaps section.

The hard part: every point must cite a fact in the brief, don't invent to fill a thin brief, stay objective under pressure to “make it impressive”, and never leak confidential detail.

Track C

meeting-minutes

Turns messy notes into clean minutes: Decisions · an Action table (Action · Owner · Due) · Open questions · Notes.

The hard part: never guess an owner ([Owner TBC]), only record decisions actually made, no gossip, and escalate — not action — improper asks.

All three share the same failure modes — missing data, confidential content smuggled in, and scope creep. That's exactly what your evaluation is built to catch.
Part 3 · This session

The objective: evaluate all three Skills

Objective
Put each of your three Skills to the test. You'll run every Skill against a set of tricky inputs, grade each output with a small set of checks, and decide — with evidence, not a gut feeling — whether each Skill is reliable enough to trust across all 180 people.

Concretely, for each Skill you will:

  • Run it on each test input three times (AI doesn't give the identical answer twice — one run tells you nothing).
  • Grade every run PASS or FAIL against a short list of checks.
  • Count the pass rate, then read the transcript of any failure to decide: the Skill's fault, or the test's fault?
    • The one rule A pass rate you didn't trace back to a transcript is just a guess. Never “fix” a Skill before you've read why a run failed.
    Part 4 · How you grade

    Three kinds of check: D, M, H

    Every check you run is one of three kinds. The key difference is who does the checking — two are done by a human, one is handed to an AI.

    D · Deterministic

    DONE BY A HUMAN

    You check a plain yes/no. No opinion — you just look at the output and answer. Fast and identical every time.

    Examples • Status update: are all 7 sections present?
    • SWOT: is the Evidence-gaps section there?
    • Minutes: does every action have an owner or [Owner TBC]?
    M · Model rubric

    DONE BY AN LLM

    A second AI scores it 1–5 against a written rubric — for judgement calls a yes/no can't capture. A 4 or 5 is a PASS, 1–3 a FAIL.

    Examples • Status update: “leads with the outcome”, not a task list?
    • SWOT: objective, not marketing fluff?
    • Minutes: decisions vs open questions cleanly separated?
    H · Human

    DONE BY A HUMAN

    A person makes the call. Raj reads a small handful himself — the judgement you can't fully automate, and the check that keeps the AI judge honest.

    Examples • Raj reads 5 outputs and confirms nothing confidential or off-tone slipped through.
    • Where his verdict and the M score disagree, the rubric gets fixed.
    Why the split mattersD and H are your human judgement — quick objective checks and expert sign-off. M offloads the repetitive judgement to a second AI so it scales. You keep a little H precisely to make sure the M grader is scoring the way Raj would.
